# ONE GOOD PART — texto del libro

Texto final en inglés. Esqueleto y tabla de control en

## Bloques fijos para generar ilustraciones

Estos dos bloques van **idénticos, palabra por palabra, en cada uno de los 40
prompts**. Sin esto, Canva le cambia la cara, el pelo y la ropa a los personajes
entre imágenes y el libro se ve roto.

**STYLE TOKEN**

> STYLE: cinematic cel-shaded animated feature background art in the tradition of
> Studio Ghibli and Cartoon Saloon — clean confident shapes with soft painted
> lighting, visible shafts of light cutting through dust in the air, warm lamp glow
> against cool blue shadow, rich atmospheric depth, layered foreground and
> background, muted natural palette, expressive but never messy. Absolutely no
> text, letters, signs or writing anywhere in the image.

**CHARACTER BLOCK**

> Professor Halden Rooke is a thin man in his mid-seventies with wild
> shoulder-length white hair, a short white beard, small round wire glasses and
> deep lines around his eyes.
> Nia Serrano is a seventeen-year-old girl with light brown skin, straight dark
> brown hair in a high ponytail, a white shirt with rolled sleeves and an
> olive-green canvas backpack.

**Regla de conteo:** cada escena declara explícitamente cuántas personas hay
("exactly one girl and no one else"). Canva duplica personajes si no se lo decís.

# Chapter 1 — The Burst Pipe

*Lisboa, Portugal · 2026 · presente*

### Página 0 — 62 palabras

Nia had a key to the workshop, but she had never needed it before eight. That
morning she **showed up** at seven, because Professor Rooke had promised her the
last three tapes by Friday, and Professor Rooke had never once kept a promise
about time. She put the key in the lock. Water came out under the door and onto
her shoes.

### Página 1 — 99 palabras

Inside, everything was floating. Tools, receipts, a cardboard box of cassette
tapes turning grey at the edges. The whole room smelled of wet paper and old oil.

"Pipe went in the night," Rooke said from the stairs. He was barefoot, in
yesterday's shirt. "I heard it at four. I told myself it was rain."

Nia lifted the box. The wet cardboard **fell apart** in her hands and the tapes
went into the water, one after another, like something being dropped on purpose.

Her mother's handwriting on the labels had already run.

"We can't **clean** this **up** with towels," she said.

### Página 2 — 64 palabras · ilustración A

Rooke started **throwing away** the ruined reels, fast, not looking at them.

"Stop."

"They're gone, Nia."

"You don't get to throw them away. Not you."

He stopped. He stood in the water in his bare feet for a long moment.

"I knew about that pipe," he said. "I've known since March. I should **own up**
to that before you hear it from someone else."

### Página 3 — 59 palabras · ilustración B

He didn't apologise again. He walked to the back wall and pulled a grey
tarpaulin off something tall.

"What is that."

"**Hold on.** Let me say it properly." He wiped his hands on his shirt. "It
works. It has worked for forty years. I've never told anyone, and I'm too old
now to **figure out** a good reason why."

### Página 4 — 61 palabras · ilustración C

He **pulled out** a small brass wheel and set it on the wet bench. A crack ran
clean across the teeth.

"Water got it. Without this, the machine jumps far and lands badly. Twelve hours
is precision. I can't do precision."

"So we **give up**."

"So we **set off** tonight. Kyoto. The man who cut this one is twenty years old."

· own up · hold on · figure out · pull out · give up · set off ✓ 10/10

# Chapter 2 — The Kyoto Workshop

*Kioto, Japón · 1889 · pasado*

### Página 0 — 61 palabras

The machine did not land them in a workshop. It landed them in a lane behind it,
in cold that went straight through Nia's jacket, two hours before the sun.
Somewhere close, a dog was complaining. Rooke counted the coins in his palm —
eleven of them, brown and thin — and said the thing she would hear all week:
"We'll **get by**."

### Página 1 — 97 palabras

By first light they had a problem that had nothing to do with time travel: they
looked wrong. Rooke bought two used jackets off a man unloading a cart, and Nia
**tried on** the smaller one behind a stack of crates while he stood guard,
badly.

"The backpack goes," he said. "If you want to **blend in**, the backpack goes."

"It has the notebook in it."

"Then carry the notebook."

They walked out into a street of wooden houses and paper windows, and Nia
understood that she had never once been properly far from home.

### Página 2 — 66 palabras · ilustración A

They **warmed up** at a food stall, a bowl each, standing in the road because
there was nowhere to sit. The man serving them said something Nia did not catch
and laughed at his own joke anyway. When Rooke asked, in bad Japanese, where the
Mizuno workshop was, an old woman at the next stall **pointed** it **out** with
her chin and did not stop eating.

### Página 3 — 57 palabras · ilustración B

On the way they **ran into** a work crew raising a wooden pole, arguing about
cable, while an ox cart went round them without slowing.

"Electricity," Rooke said, delighted. "They're building the first tram line in
the country. Right now. This week."

"And we're here **looking for** a gear."

"We're here looking for a very good gear."

### Página 4 — 62 palabras · ilustración C

They **took off** their shoes at the step. Inside, a young man was bent over a
low bench, filing a wheel no bigger than a coin.

"Two days," said Sato Kenji, without looking up. "Don't **hurry** me **up**.
Fast work is bad work."

Nia **wrote down** every measurement he gave them. Three streets away, the
machine was already too hot to touch.

# Chapter 3 — What We Took

*Kioto, Japón · 1889 · pasado*

### Página 0 — 66 palabras

Kenji went out for charcoal at nine. At the door he stopped, thought about it,
and said the sentence that would cost him everything: "**Look after** the bench
for ten minutes."

Nia said yes. She was seventeen and someone had trusted her with something, and
it felt good, and that was the whole of it. She has thought about those ten
minutes every day since.

### Página 1 — 98 palabras

Four minutes in, a man came through the back door in a hurry — the master's son,
sleeves already rolled, a ledger under his arm and no time for anyone. He looked
at the cloth on the bench, then at Rooke.

"The finished one? It's paid for?"

"It isn't," Rooke said, and then, because the machine was three streets away and
getting hotter, "but it can be."

The son had **mixed up** two orders that morning and would not notice for a
week. He named a price. Rooke **handed over** every coin he had, and some of
Nia's.

### Página 2 — 63 palabras · ilustración A

Nia **picked up** the piece. It was warm from the light and heavier than it
looked, and the teeth were so fine she could not count them with her eyes.

"Put it back," she said. She didn't know why.

"It's ours. He sold it."

"**Put** it **back**, Professor."

Somewhere behind the houses, a sound started that only the two of them could
hear.

### Página 3 — 61 palabras · ilustración B

Their time **ran out** all at once, the way it always does.

"We'll **sort** it **out** when we're home," Rooke said, already moving toward
the door.

They **headed off** down the lane at a run, and Nia's bag was open the whole
way, and the notebook went from her arm to the step to the floor without anyone
hearing it land.

### Página 4 — 61 palabras · ilustración C

Kenji returned with the charcoal at twenty past nine. The cloth was open and
empty. He stood there a while, trying to remember whether he had moved it
himself.

On the floor by the step was a black notebook, full of drawings he could not
read.

"I'd **take** it **back** if I could," Nia said, a hundred and thirty-seven years
away.

# Chapter 4 — Stuck in Valparaíso

*Valparaíso, Chile · marzo de 1906 · pasado*

### Página 0 — 56 palabras

The machine **broke down** halfway. Nia felt it — a stutter, like a car changing
gear badly — and then heat, and then a smell of burning dust, and then gulls.

They were standing on a hillside above a harbour full of masts, in warm
afternoon light, in the wrong century and the wrong hemisphere. March, 1906.

### Página 1 — 95 palabras

"How do you **end up** in Chile aiming for Portugal?" Nia said.

"Three days," said Rooke, not answering, already opening the panel. "The core
has to cool. Three days and I can recalculate."

Three days with no money. They **looked around** the port for somewhere to sleep
and **turned down** the first two offers, one because of the price and one
because of the man making it. In the end they **put up with** a doorway behind a
bakery, which was warm at four in the morning and smelled of bread they could not
buy.

### Página 2 — 63 palabras · ilustración A

They worked. Nia carried crates at the fish market for a man who paid at the end
of every shift and never looked at her face. A broken cart **held** them **up**
for an hour on the second morning and cost her half a day's pay. By the third day
the other loaders had stopped watching to see if she would drop one.

### Página 3 — 60 palabras · ilustración B

Rooke worked a corner with a handkerchief spread on a crate, mending pocket
watches for sailors, and learned how you **get around** a city built on forty
hills: you pay a coin and a wooden lift hauls you up the slope on a cable.

"We could **check in** somewhere tonight," Nia said, counting the coins twice.

"We could eat instead."

### Página 4 — 67 palabras · ilustración C

On the last afternoon Rooke went to buy a spring from a watchmaker he had used
before, on a previous jump. The address was right. The shop was a boarded
doorway. He **asked around** for an hour and nobody had heard the name.

"It'll **work out**," he said, on the way back, and for the first time Nia
noticed that he said that when he was frightened.

# Chapter 5 — The Wrong Lisbon

*Lisboa, Portugal · 2026-B · presente alterado*

### Página 0 — 57 palabras

The landing was perfect. That was the thing Nia would remember: the machine
finally did exactly what it was supposed to do. Same street. Same cobbles. Same
tiles, cracked in the same place by the door.

Where the workshop had been there was a shop selling phone cases, open, lit,
busy, at nine on a Tuesday night.

### Página 1 — 92 palabras

They couldn't **get in**. A girl behind the counter said the unit had been hers
for four years and would not **let** them **in** to look at the back wall, which
was completely reasonable and did not help at all, and Rooke stood in the doorway
saying his own address at her until Nia pulled him out.

On the pavement she **looked** it **up** on her phone. No workshop. No listing.
No archived photo.

"We **came back** to the wrong one," Rooke said.

"No," said Nia. "This is the right one now."

### Página 2 — 68 palabras · ilustración A

It **turned out** he was still alive, still seventy-four, still in Lisbon. He had
spent forty years in an insurance office on Avenida Almirante Reis and retired
last spring with a clock and a card everyone had signed.

The workshop had not **shut down**. It had never opened. Nobody had ever taught
him to hold a file, and his hands, when he looked at them, were somebody else's.

### Página 3 — 65 palabras · ilustración B

Her own house was there. Her family was there. But in this version they had
**moved out** of the neighbourhood when she was six, half the street had been
**torn down** and rebuilt, and there was no Professor Rooke in her life at all —
no bench, no Saturdays, no tapes.

Forty years of somebody's work, **wiped out**, and only two people on earth could
tell.

### Página 4 — 60 palabras · ilustración C

"Somebody taught my teacher," Rooke said. He was sitting on a kerb, which she had
never once seen him do. "Walt learned it from a Japanese man who came to Europe
in the thirties. Find him and we find the hole. I need to **find out** what
happened to him."

"And if he's dead?"

"Then somewhere in 1935 he isn't."

# Chapter 6 — Finding Kenji

*Marrakech, Marruecos · 1935 · pasado*

### Página 0 — 55 palabras

It took four days to **track him down**. Ship records, a work permit, a name
written three different ways. Sato Kenji had failed his examination in 1889, left
Kyoto that winter, taken whatever a strong young man could take, and washed up,
eventually, in North Africa. He was sixty-six years old and he mended pots.

### Página 1 — 96 palabras

They **got through** the crowd in the square at the hour when the food stalls go
up and the whole place starts shouting at once, and cut into the tinsmiths' lane,
where four hundred lanterns hang from the ceiling and every man is hammering
something. The noise was a physical thing; you felt it in your teeth.

He was at the end, on a low stool. Small. Enormous forearms. Reading glasses
pushed up.

He looked at them politely and did not know them. Forty-six years had gone by,
and they had been two foreigners for three minutes.

### Página 2 — 66 palabras · ilustración A

He made them tea, pouring from a height so the mint turned over in the glass.
Rooke **brought up** Kyoto, carefully, the way you touch a bruise, and the old man
went quiet for a long moment.

Then he **opened up**. He talked for an hour. He had not talked about it in forty
years. Not to a customer, not to a wife, not to anybody.

### Página 3 — 65 palabras · ilustración B

He **went over** the examination rules from memory, still word-perfect. One
finished piece on the bench by the sixth hour. No piece, no pass.

"Nobody ever **looked into** why I failed," he said. "They just failed me."

"And after?"

He **gave in** and showed them his hands, and his tools, **worn out** and
re-handled twice. "You **carry on**. That's all there is. You carry on."

### Página 4 — 58 palabras · ilustración C

"**Stop by** tomorrow," he said. "I close at dark." Then, packing up, without
weight: "A girl left a notebook on my bench once, that same week. Strange
drawings. I kept it forty years, then gave it to an archive that collects old
trades."

Nia's mouth went dry.

"Which archive?"

"They moved. Lagos, I think. Nigeria. They took everything."

# Chapter 7 — Lagos, Level Nine

*Lagos, Nigeria · 2141 · futuro*

### Página 0 — 62 palabras

Two hundred and fifteen years forward, the machine set them down on a walkway
nine levels above the water, and Nia stopped dead and just stood there to **take
it in**: a city built in layers over a lagoon, boats moving below, gardens on the
roofs, and more people than she had ever seen at once, all of them going
somewhere.

### Página 1 — 100 palabras

Nothing here was hostile. Everything here was fast. Rooke could not **keep up**
with the walking crowd and had to hold the rail, twice, and pretend he was
admiring the view. It was the first time Nia had ever had to slow down for him.

"**Look out** —" A cargo drone came over low enough to move his hair.

A boy at a stall **plugged in** a cracked panel and it woke up, and Nia
understood something about this place before she could say it: nobody was selling
anything new. The whole level was repair, and everybody on it had a trade.

### Página 2 — 62 palabras · ilustración A

The old fish market had **turned into** a repair floor: shelves of sorted parts
from the ground to the ceiling, three people working three different benches, a
queue of customers with broken things in their arms, waiting their turn.

"You'll **get used to** the noise," a woman told Nia, not unkindly. "Or you
won't. Both are fine." And went back to work.

### Página 3 — 65 palabras · ilustración B

You had to **sign up** to enter the archive, and then you had to **line up** like
everyone else. A woman at the door **handed out** numbered tokens and told people
how long they would wait.

"**Speak up**, uncle," she said to Rooke. "I can't hear you."

They waited two hours. Nobody complained. It was clearly normal, and Nia found
that she did not mind.

### Página 4 — 56 palabras · ilustración C

On the way out Nia stopped at a shelf near the entrance, behind glass,
unlabelled: forty or fifty small mechanisms, all broken, all waiting.

"Everything here gets fixed," she said. "So why is there a whole shelf of things
nobody can fix?"

Rooke looked at the shelf for a long time and did not answer her.

# Chapter 8 — My Own Handwriting

*Lagos, Nigeria · 2141 · futuro*

### Página 0 — 70 palabras

The archive was cold and badly lit on purpose. Aisles of grey boxes going up
further than the light reached, and one long table where you were allowed to open
one box at a time, with cotton gloves, while somebody watched you do it.
Everything in the building had been **backed up** twice, the archivist said, and
once on paper, because paper is the only copy that survives being forgotten.

### Página 1 — 96 palabras

They asked for everything donated by a tinsmith from Marrakesh, and **came
across** it in the third box, under a tin cup and a folded apron: a black
notebook, two hundred and fifty years old, the cover gone soft as cloth. Somebody
had written a catalogue number on it in white ink.

Nia **looked through** it with the gloves on, one page at a time, and could
barely **make out** the pencil.

It was her handwriting. Not similar. Hers. The measurements she had taken from
Kenji's bench, in her own hand, catalogued and numbered by strangers.

### Página 2 — 56 palabras · ilustración A

She **held back** in front of the archivist, which took everything she had, and
put both gloved hands flat on the table until it passed.

"Now it **adds up**," Rooke said, very quietly, beside her. "He kept it. Of
course he kept it. It was the only thing he had left from the week he lost."

### Página 3 — 60 palabras · ilustración B

Between them they **pieced together** what had actually broken. Not history. A
line of teaching: Kenji had never become a master, so he never taught, so nobody
carried the method forward, so the shelf by the door was full.

"**Sum it up** for me in one line," said Nia.

"We stole a man's future and spent it on a gear."

### Página 4 — 59 palabras · ilustración C

They **ruled out** going back to Kyoto empty-handed; you cannot un-take a thing.

"We give him a better one," Rooke said. "One good part, on that bench, before the
examiner walks in. The measurements are here. We only need a machine that can cut
them."

The archivist **set** the box **aside** for them and asked no questions at all.

# Chapter 9 — One Good Part

*Detroit, EE.UU. · 1957 · pasado → Kioto, 1889*

### Página 0 — 58 palabras

There was exactly one moment in history when that gear had been cut by machine,
and it was a parts shop on Michigan Avenue in the winter of 1957. Rooke knew the
address without checking. He had swept that floor when he was eleven years old,
and again at fourteen, and again every summer after that.

### Página 1 — 100 palabras

The owner was twenty-four years old, in overalls and a cloth cap, and his name
was Walt Rooke. Halden had to look at the floor for a moment before he could
speak at all.

He could not say a single true word to him. He asked for the loan of a lathe like
any other customer and paid for the hour in cash.

Walt **showed them around** the floor anyway, because he showed everyone around,
and told Nia to **try out** the small lathe first. "She pulls left. Everybody
learns that the hard way. Now you don't have to," he said.

### Página 2 — 67 palabras · ilustración A

The first cut was wrong and they **started over**. The second was worse.

"I **messed** it **up**," Nia said.

"So did I. Twice. Go again."

On the third she **straightened out** the mounting with her hands instead of the
tool, the way nobody teaches you, and the swarf came off the cut in one long
bright ribbon. Her hands were steadier than his had been at her age.

### Página 3 — 63 palabras · ilustración B

The power was **cut off** at six, the way it was every night on that block, and
they finished by lamp.

"Can I **count on** you to hold it steady?" Rooke said, and she did.

They **pulled it off**. Walt turned the piece over twice under the light and
said, "That'll outlive you" — a sentence Halden had first heard at ten years old.

### Página 4 — 56 palabras · ilustración C

Kyoto, 1889, nine minutes past nine in the morning. The workshop was empty. The
cloth was open.

Rooke **put** the piece **down** in the middle of it and squared it with one
finger.

Footsteps in the corridor, unhurried, official.

"Now," said Nia, and they **got away** through the back before the door slid open
behind them.

# Chapter 10 — Back on Time

*Lisboa, Portugal · 2026 · presente restaurado*

### Página 0 — 56 palabras

Same street. The workshop was there, door propped open, and the whole place
smelled of oil and wet wood.

So did the water. They had arrived twelve hours after the pipe, not before it,
because they never could have arrived before it. That part they did not fix, and
were never going to be able to.

### Página 1 — 100 palabras

The box **turned up** on the top shelf, where it had always been: a small wooden
box from Japan that Rooke had never opened because it was his teacher's and not
his. Forty years he had dusted around it.

Inside was her notebook, a hundred and thirty-seven years old, and a letter dated
1904, signed *Sato Kenji, master*, addressed to *the girl who left this on my
bench*.

He had passed. He had taught. And that line of teaching, unbroken, is what put
Halden Rooke in this room. Outside, the street **settled down** as the last of
the trucks left.

### Página 2 — 61 palabras · ilustración A

Of the three tapes, two were gone for good. The third dried out and played, and
for thirty seconds Nia's mother said something ordinary about dinner in a kitchen
that no longer exists, and it **brought back** all of it at once, the whole
kitchen.

"You don't **get over** it," Rooke said from the doorway. "You get better at
carrying it."

### Página 3 — 57 palabras · ilustración B

They **wrapped up** the cleaning at two in the morning. Nia had three weeks of
school to **catch up on** and did not care.

Rooke **put away** his tools one at a time, slowly, in the order he had kept them
for forty years, and did not take a single one of them out again that night.

### Página 4 — 66 palabras · ilustración C

"You'll **take over** the shop," he said. "I've decided. Kenji **passed on** what
he knew and it reached me, so I'm passing it on, and that's the whole of the
trade. **Take** it **up** properly. Not just Saturdays."

She was already outside, measuring the doorway for the new sign.

"It doesn't say Rooke anymore."

"No. It says Rooke and Serrano. Get in here and hold this."
